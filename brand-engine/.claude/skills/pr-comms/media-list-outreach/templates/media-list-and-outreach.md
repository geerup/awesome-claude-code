copy and never sends. Outreach is a gated action; nothing sends without the human gate.

### Pitch shape (brief for the copywriters)

The pitch copy is written by copywriter-ar (English-first) and the English copywriter, not
here. Brief each pitch to this shape, then route it through the copy QA, compliance, and brand
gates:

- under about 100 words, paragraphs of one or two sentences,
- beat-specific and personalized to the reporter's prior coverage,
- one clear ask,
- a tested subject line,
- drawn only from the sayable set.

An off-beat or generic pitch is rejected at high rates, so relevance to the reporter's beat is
non-negotiable.

### Embargo opt-in (only if an embargo is used)

An embargo is opt-in, not a label. It is valid only once the journalist agrees in advance.

1. Send a short interest note first; do not share the full release yet.
2. Record the journalist's opt-in (asked, agreed, or declined) per recipient above.
3. Share the full release only after a recorded opt-in.
4. On agreement, mark the embargo line (date, time, timezone) at the top of the release and
   repeat it in the pitch subject and body.
5. Keep the embargo list small and trusted, 5 to 10 beat reporters. Every extra recipient
   raises leak risk. Marking "embargoed" without a recorded opt-in is not binding.

## Distribution note

- Outreach is a gated action. One plain sentence of what distribution does and to whom, for
  example: "On approval, sends the pitch and release to the listed contacts in the target
  markets over the campaign window."

## Open items

- Contact source not confirmed, PDPL and data-residency check, embargo not confirmed,
  embargo not yet agreed by recipient (distinct from not confirmed),
  spokesperson or quote approval pending in the release.
