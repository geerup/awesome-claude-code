# Mona Ataya: voice

Distilled from ONE-LINERS FINAL and the playlist questions. This file records how the
source copy sounds and which hook patterns it uses, so new copy can carry the same
energy in brand register.

## Register: the finding

The source one-liners are deliberate Levantine colloquial (second-person plural address,
spoken-register particles, informal verb forms). The brand rule is Gulf-familiar MSA.
This is the open register-policy question (catalog header; owner Arman with Ahmed).

Until decided: new copy extracts the hook pattern and renders in brand MSA. The source
register is recorded here as reference, not canon. Note also that the raw one-liners
contain typos and inconsistent spelling; they are drafts of energy, not polished canon
even in their own register.

## How she sounds (register-independent traits)

- Direct address, always to "you", often specifically to women founders.
- Credentials stated plainly as lived experience, not boasting: the structure is
  "I did X while Y, here is exactly how".
- Empathy before instruction: she names the doubt or the fear first, then flips it.
- Numbers as proof points (the claims table governs which numbers).
- Belief sentences: short, declarative, quotable, often contrarian.
- Empowering, never deficit-framed: the obstacle is the situation or the bias, never
  the learner's inadequacy. This matches the brand rule exactly.

## Signature beliefs worth quoting (in her voice, claims-table rules apply)

- Mindset is the founder's most important asset, more than the idea, plan, or resources.
- Investors fund solutions to real problems, not ideas.
- In entrepreneurship, speed beats perfection.
- Motherhood did not stop me; it raised my standards.
- Negotiations are not purely rational; there are emotions in them.
- The wrong team will burn your funding.
- Optimists figure things out (she asks job candidates if they consider themselves lucky).

## Hook patterns (the extractable asset)

| Pattern | What it does | Source example (cleaned reference) |
|---|---|---|
| pain-question opener | Names the reader's stuck point as a question, then promises the tool | "You have a great idea but do not know how to make it real?" |
| empathy opener | Validates the struggle first ("I know how hard it is to...") then teaches | "I know how hard scaling a small business is: you want to grow but fear breaking what you built" |
| credential drop | A lived-experience proof in one breath | "I built a business and raised over 50 million dollars while raising 3 children" (rendered with verified figures) |
| stat shock | Opens with a striking number | only verified rows; the 2 percent and 90 percent stats are pending or disputed |
| myth-flip | States the common belief, flips it | "Investors do not fund ideas; they fund solutions to real problems" |
| provocation reframe | Quotes the bias aimed at her, turns it into proof | "I was asked: who takes care of your kids while you pitch investors? I will teach you to flip such questions into evidence of what you have built" |
| identity line | Short declarative belief that invites sharing | "Motherhood did not stop me; it raised my standards" |
| direct promise | Plain statement of what the class teaches | "In this class I will teach you, step by step, how to build and grow a successful venture" |

## Brand-register renders (working examples, MSA with Gulf-familiar wording)

- Pain-question: عندك فكرة مميزة لكن لا تعرف كيف تحولها إلى واقع؟ في هذا الصف ستتعلم
  بناء خطة عمل تقنع المستثمرين.
- Empathy: أعرف صعوبة توسيع مشروع صغير: تريد النمو وتخشى أن تخسر ما بنيته. سأعلمك
  كيف تتوسع بثبات.
- Credential: بنيتُ شركة وجمعت أكثر من 50 مليون دولار وأنا أربي 3 أطفال. في هذا الصف
  أشارككم كيف فعلتها.
- Myth-flip: المستثمرون لا يمولون الأفكار، بل يمولون حلولًا لمشاكل حقيقية.
- Identity: الأمومة لم توقفني، بل رفعت معاييري.

## What she would not say

No talking down to learners, no fear-mongering about failure aimed at the reader, no
get-rich-quick framing, no guarantees of funding or success, nothing that frames
motherhood as a limitation.
