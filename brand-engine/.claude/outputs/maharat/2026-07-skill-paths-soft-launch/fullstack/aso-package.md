## Section 1. Keyword research: intent map and keyword candidates

This section documents the reasoning and candidate terms. Final keyword selection per store and
per locale awaits live data from an approved ASO research tool (AppTweak or Sensor Tower, see
section 9). The candidates below are organized by intent theme and ready for that tool pass.

### 1.1 Research framing

Arabic-first. The primary store presence is Arabic; the English listing is additive, not the
anchor. Keywords serve two user intents simultaneously: discovery by people who want to learn a
skill or build a habit, and discovery by people looking for an Arabic-language self-development
app. Both intents are live and addressable in the stores. The Skill Paths format is
Duolingo-like, so micro-learning, habit, and streak vocabulary is on-theme and likely to
index against real search behavior.

Store character limits shape the keyword field:

- App Store: 100-character keyword field (the hidden field), plus the title (30 characters) and
  subtitle (30 characters) all act as indexed terms. No keyword repetition between fields.
- Google Play: no dedicated keyword field. Title (50 characters), short description (80
  characters), and long description (4,000 characters) are the indexable surfaces. Keywords
  must appear naturally in copy, not in a list.

## Section 2. Store listing optimization: title, subtitle, description, keyword field

All strings below are draft direction. They route to copywriter-ar (Arabic) and copywriter-en
(English), then through arabic-copy-qa and english-copy-qa, then brand-qa-reviewer, before they
enter any store field. The directions here set the intent and character budget, not the final copy.

### 2.1 Store title

Character limits: App Store title 30 characters, Google Play title 50 characters.

The title must carry the brand name and the format benefit. It is the highest-weighted ranking
signal and the first thing a user reads on the listing.

Direction for Arabic title (App Store, 30 characters):

- Must include the brand name (مهارات).
- Must signal the format benefit: daily learning, skill building, or the small-step-a-day angle.
- Must not name a specific Skill Path or invent a product feature.
- Draft direction for copywriter-ar: brand name plus a short, confident benefit phrase that fits
  30 characters. Example shape (not final, routes to QA): مهارات: تعلم بخطوة كل يوم
  (This is an angle anchor, not approved copy. copywriter-ar owns the final string.)

Direction for Arabic title (Google Play, 50 characters):

- More room. Brand name plus a stronger benefit phrase is possible.
- Draft direction: brand name, benefit phrase, optionally a format signal (daily, skill, bite-sized).
  Example shape (not final): مهارات: طور نفسك بخطوة يومية وابن مهارة حقيقية
  (Routes to copywriter-ar and QA.)

Direction for English title (App Store, 30 characters):

- Brand name (Maharat) plus short benefit.
- Example shape (not final): Maharat: Build Skills Daily
  (Routes to copywriter-en and english-copy-qa.)

Direction for English title (Google Play, 50 characters):

- Example shape (not final): Maharat: Daily Skills, One Step at a Time
  (Routes to copywriter-en and english-copy-qa.)

### 2.2 Store subtitle (App Store only, 30 characters)

The subtitle is a second indexed field and the immediate value proposition below the title.

Direction for Arabic subtitle:

- Should not repeat the title word for word.
- Reinforce the streak and habit mechanic, or the early-access pull, within 30 characters.
- Example shape (not final): ابن عادة التعلم، خطوة واحدة كل يوم
  (Routes to copywriter-ar. Must stay at or under 30 characters after finalization.)

Direction for English subtitle:

- Example shape (not final): Learn a new skill, one step a day
  (Routes to copywriter-en. Must stay at or under 30 characters.)

Note: Google Play has no subtitle field. The short description (80 characters) plays the same
functional role and is addressed in section 2.3.

### 2.3 Store description

App Store long description (4,000 characters maximum). Google Play long description (4,000
characters, HTML formatting allowed). Google Play short description (80 characters, appears in
search results before full description, no HTML).

These are direction briefs for copywriter-ar (Arabic primary) and copywriter-en (English).

Structure direction for Arabic long description (App Store and Google Play):

Opening (above the fold, first 160 to 200 characters visible before "more"):
- Lead with the core benefit and the angle: one small step a day builds a real skill that stays.
  Empowering framing. The hero is the learner who can build a habit that fits their day.
- Must not name specific Skill Path titles or a content lineup.
- Must not imply certificate accreditation.
- Must not name a launch date or promo price.
- Must signal: Arabic-first, gamified, daily habit, early access now available.

Body paragraphs (after "more"):
- Paragraph 1: what the format delivers. Bite-sized, daily, habit-building. The streak mechanic
  as a benefit (consistency made visible), described by function not by invented feature name.
- Paragraph 2: who it is for. A busy Arabic-speaking adult who wants to grow but struggles to
  find time for long courses. Empowering. Not deficit-framed.
- Paragraph 3: why now. Early access is open. Get in at the start of something new. The
  early-access framing, not a countdown to an unconfirmed date.
- Paragraph 4 (optional if character budget allows): the wider Maharat ecosystem context.
  Masterclasses, guides, certificates of completion (not accredited), a community of Arabic
  learners. This paragraph grounds the new format in an established platform.

Closing:
- A single clear call to action. Download and start today. Or: join early access now.
  The language routes to copywriter-ar and must match the gate in market.

Keyword embedding guidance for Arabic Google Play description:
- تطوير الذات and تعلم مهارة: appear naturally in the opening and body, once each.
- بناء العادات and تعلم يومي: in the habit paragraph.
- تطبيق عربي and منصة تعليمية: in the body or closing.
- No keyword stuffing. Each term must read naturally in the sentence.

Structure direction for English long description (App Store and Google Play):

Same four-paragraph structure as Arabic. The English version is not a literal translation; it
carries the same intent in English with the same voice: plain, confident, empowering, no hype.

Keyword embedding guidance for English Google Play description:
- "self improvement", "skill building", "daily habit": in the opening and habit paragraphs.
- "micro learning", "bite-sized learning": in the format paragraph.
- "Arabic learning app" or "learn in Arabic": once in the body (addresses the Arabic-first
  positioning in the English listing).

Google Play short description direction (Arabic, 80 characters):
- Single empowering sentence. The benefit in one breath. No invented titles.
- Example shape (not final): تعلم مهارة جديدة بخطوة يومية واحدة. تطبيق مهارات للتطوير الذاتي.
  (Routes to copywriter-ar. 80-character limit is strict. Verify character count.)

Google Play short description direction (English, 80 characters):
- Example shape (not final): Build a real skill, one step a day. The Arabic-first learning app.
  (Routes to copywriter-en. Verify character count.)

## Section 4. Store A/B experiment plan

App Store: Product Page Optimization (PPO), up to 3 treatment variants per test, minimum 90
days recommended but the soft-launch window is 14 days. Note: App Store PPO requires Apple's
allocation of test traffic; a 14-day window limits statistical confidence. These experiments are
set up now and treated as learning vehicles for the full launch, not decisive in this flight.

Google Play: Store Listing Experiments, up to 3 variants, available within Google Play Console.
Play's experiment tool offers quicker traffic routing and is better suited to a 14-day sprint.

### 4.1 Experiment 1: Subtitle test (App Store) and Short Description test (Google Play)

Variable tested: the first line of value proposition visible before the user taps "more."

Hypothesis: a subtitle or short description that leads with the streak and habit benefit ("one
step a day") will drive a higher tap-through rate to the install action than one that leads with
the Arabic-first positioning claim. Tied to the strategy-artifact angle: the audience's core
pain is time and consistency, not language platform discovery.

Control: subtitle or short description leading with format and habit language.
Variant A: subtitle or short description leading with Arabic-first and cultural credibility.
Variant B: subtitle or short description leading with the early-access framing and urgency.

Success measure: store listing conversion rate (tap to install), linked to the campaign primary
success metric (app installs attributable to the campaign, per the strategy-artifact).

QA note: all three variants route through the copy QA path before the experiment is configured.

### 4.2 Experiment 2: Lead screenshot test (App Store PPO and Google Play)

Variable tested: the visual message of frame 1 in the screenshot sequence.

Hypothesis: a frame 1 that leads with an abstract streak-progression visual (the small-step
motif) will outperform a frame 1 that leads with the brand mark and benefit headline alone,
because the streak visual communicates format before the user reads the copy.

Control: frame 1 as specced in section 3.3 (brand lock-up, headline overlay, emerald accent).
Variant A: frame 1 replaced by the streak/progression visual (frame 2 concept promoted to lead).

Success measure: store listing conversion rate (tap to install).

Note: this experiment is easy to configure with assets already in the 5-frame sequence. The
variant is not a new asset; it is a resequencing of the existing creative plan.

### 4.3 Experiment 3: Title variant test (Google Play only, 14-day sprint)

Variable tested: the Google Play title framing.

Hypothesis: a title that surfaces the gamification or streak signal alongside the brand name
will index better for habit-and-streak search queries and drive stronger installs from that
intent cluster, compared to a title that surfaces only brand and skill-building language.

Control: title with brand name and skill-building benefit phrase.
Variant A: title with brand name and gamification or streak signal.

Success measure: keyword ranking change on habit and streak terms plus install rate from
organic store search. This feeds the full-launch keyword strategy beyond this flight.

### 4.4 Experiment execution notes

- All variants route through the same copy QA path as the control before configuration.
- All experiments are set up in the store console before the 2026-07-01 start date.
- Setting up experiments requires App Store Connect and Google Play Console access (open item,
  section 9).
- Results from the 14-day window are shared with analytics-reporter against the success metric.
  The data scope is limited; interpretation for the full-launch strategy is the primary value.
- No experiment adjusts the listing outside the approved variant set without a new gate cycle.

## Section 5. Reviews response policy

### 5.1 Governing voice and principles

Responses to ratings and reviews in the stores represent Maharat publicly. Every response uses
the Maharat voice: plain, confident, empowering, never defensive, never hype. Arabic-first
where the review is in Arabic. English response where the review is in English. No em dashes,
no tatweel, Western numerals. RTL-correct for Arabic responses.

A review response is a customer-facing asset. It goes through brand-qa-reviewer judgment
before any template is approved for use. Individual responses to specific reviews do not go
through a gate cycle each time, but they must follow this policy and stay within the templates.

### 5.2 Response categories

Category 1: positive review, 4 or 5 stars.

Response goal: acknowledge warmly, reinforce the empowering brand identity, invite continued
engagement. Keep it short (2 to 3 sentences). Never hype. Never manufactured enthusiasm.

Direction for copywriter-ar (Arabic template):
- Thank the user briefly and specifically if the review mentions a feature.
- Reinforce the benefit: you are building something real, one step at a time.
- Invite them back: there is more to come, the path continues.
- No roadmap, no unannounced plans, no launch date hint.

Direction for copywriter-en (English template):
- Same structure, same constraints, plain and confident.

What to escalate: a 5-star review that asks a pricing question or a subscription question.
Route to the support team; do not answer pricing in a public store response.

Category 2: neutral review, 3 stars. Often a mixed experience.

Response goal: acknowledge the specific feedback genuinely, state what is being done or invite
the user to reach the support channel for resolution. Do not be defensive.

Direction:
- Name the specific friction the reviewer described if it is clear.
- State one concrete thing: "we have passed this to the team" or "reach us at [support
  channel] and we will look into this with you."
- No promise of a feature fix, no roadmap hint, no unconfirmed improvement.
- Never argue or explain away the complaint.

What to escalate: any review that mentions a payment issue, a data or privacy concern, or a
complaint that might indicate a legal exposure. Do not respond in the store; route to the
legal and support team and note the review URL.

Category 3: negative review, 1 or 2 stars.

Response goal: de-escalate, acknowledge, redirect to the support channel. Never match the
negative tone. Never argue. Never make promises about unconfirmed fixes.

Direction:
- Open with acknowledgment: the experience the reviewer described is not what the user
  should have.
- One sentence: invite them to the support channel with a clear path (email or in-app support
  link, whichever is confirmed and live).
- Close with the brand promise, once, briefly: "we are here to make this right."
- No defensive justification. No "actually this is how it works."
- No roadmap or feature promise.

What never to say in any response:
- Do not mention or hint at accreditation.
- Do not name an unannounced Skill Path title or content plan.
- Do not name a launch date, a promo, or a price unless it is currently live and confirmed.
- Do not use "coming soon" for any feature or path not publicly announced.
- Do not use em dashes or Eastern Arabic numerals.

What to escalate immediately (to Ahmed or to the support team lead, same-day):
- Any review alleging data misuse or a privacy violation.
- Any review alleging a financial charge dispute not initiated by the user.
- Any review that includes personal information the user may not have intended to make public
  (names, phone numbers, identifying details). Do not repeat this information in the response.
- Any review that appears to be a coordinated attack (multiple similar low-star reviews in a
  short window). Flag the pattern, do not engage each review individually.

### 5.3 Review solicitation policy

When to ask for a review: after a positive in-app moment, for example after the user completes
a streak milestone or finishes their first lesson. Never ask after a friction event.

The in-app prompt uses the native OS review request API (SKStoreReviewRequest on iOS,
ReviewManager on Android). No custom review-gate that filters by predicted sentiment; that
practice violates both stores' policies.

Timing: configure the review prompt to fire no more than once per 365 days per user, after a
positive milestone. Exact trigger event resolves with the product team and data-tracking-engineer
when the in-app event map is available.

### 5.4 Rating and review KPIs

Track and report to analytics-reporter:

- Average store rating, weekly, during the soft-launch window.
- Review volume by star tier, weekly.
- Response rate (the share of reviews that receive a response within 48 hours).
- Escalation count by category (data/privacy, payment, coordinated attack).

These feed the performance-readout in stream 8 and inform the full-launch listing strategy.

## Section 6. Performance-marketer coordination

ASO and paid app acquisition reinforce one another. This section states the coordination
contract so the paid and organic store listings work together.

Store listing as landing page: the App Store and Google Play listing is the organic landing
page for every paid ad that drives an app install. The creative consistency rule is: the visual
and copy language in the paid ad and the first screenshot in the store listing should match
closely enough that the user does not experience a jarring transition. The paid creative team
and the store creative must share the same angle (small-step and streak motif, brand constants).

Keyword coordination: paid search terms on Google UAC (Universal App Campaigns) and Apple
Search Ads should overlap with the organic keyword targets where volume supports it. Share the
keyword map (section 1) with performance-marketer so both paid and organic are pulling on the
same intent clusters.

Apple Search Ads: if Apple Search Ads is in the media plan, the exact-match keyword list for
the Arabic listing should prioritize the Theme A and Theme B Arabic terms from section 1.2.
The store listing copy must contain those terms or near-variants to maximize relevance scores.

Deep linking: if paid ads link directly to the app download (not a gate page), the install
attribution source must be set up with data-tracking-engineer before the flight starts. Do not
use personal data in URL parameters or tracking tokens (CLAUDE.md hard rule).
