## 1. Sale sequence structure: how many emails, how far apart, what each does

### How many sale emails before fatigue

A 3-to-4-email sale arc generates roughly 3 to 5 times more conversions than a single
promotional send, based on multi-campaign ecommerce data (source: Bloomreach / Shopify
benchmarks, referenced in industry search synthesis, June 2026). A single last-call send
leaves the majority of potential conversions unclaimed. Three is the canonical floor for a
timed promotional window.

Promotional sends beyond 3 in a short window tip into fatigue. In this campaign E5, E6, and
E7 are 3 promotional sends over 3 days, which is the right ceiling for a premium brand and a
14-day arc that has already warmed the list across E1 to E4. Going to 4 or 5 sale emails
within that window would push the unsubscribe rate for a premium-positioned subscription,
where the benchmark sits at 0.2 to 0.6 percent for retail or subscription promotions before
fatigue signals emerge (source: Omnisend and Flowium benchmark research, 2025 to 2026).

### What each email does and why the order matters

The 3-email arc maps to 3 psychological moments, and each email has one job.

E5 (announce, 2 or 3 days before deadline): the offer statement. Leads with value, closes
with the deal. The reader does not yet feel pressure; they feel informed. Body structure:
(a) a single compelling value line (the skill, the library, the summer moment), (b) the offer
stated clearly (percentage, code, deadline), (c) one CTA to plans. The announce email
typically carries the highest open rate in the sequence because it is news. Do not waste it on
urgency language; use it for value density.

E6 (ending tomorrow, 1 day before deadline): the time-awareness email. Shorter body than E5.
Restates the offer in one sentence, then makes the time concrete. "The offer closes tomorrow."
No new value arguments. The reader already knows the value; the only new information is the
clock. This email's open rate is driven by the subject line because many readers are looking
for the deadline confirmation after opening E5.

E7 (last chance, day of deadline): the shortest email in the arc. Often 2 to 3 short
paragraphs or even less. The deadline is the whole message. Body: restate what they get in
one sentence, state the deadline precisely ("The offer ends at midnight, [day] [date]."),
single CTA. Do not apologize, do not add new offers, do not stack copy. This email converts
the highest share of the arc's revenue relative to its length. MailerLite field research cited
in a 2026 synthesis found genuine deadline-driven urgency ("Workshop starts in 2 hours")
converts 35 percent better than generic "Last chance!" phrasing, because specificity makes the
deadline feel real.

### Optimal spacing over the roughly 14-day arc

The arc the strategy-artifact proposes (E5 on day 11, E6 on day 13, E7 on day 14 of the 14-day
window) is structurally correct. The spacing pattern that emerges from ecommerce and edtech
sequence data (Instantly.ai, monday.com benchmark synthesis, 2025 to 2026):

- Early arc (E1 to E4): 2 to 3-day gaps, relationship-building cadence.
- Sale opens (E5): placed at least 2 days before the close. This gives the reader a genuine
  decision window without being trapped by an imminent deadline on first hearing the news.
- E5 to E6 gap: 2 days. Long enough that E6 does not feel like spam, short enough to maintain
  momentum.
- E6 to E7 gap: 1 day. The tightest gap in the arc, appropriate only because E7 signals a
  real, same-day close. Anything tighter than 1 day (sending both in the same morning) is
  spam territory unless there is a genuine intraday deadline.
- E7 send time: morning local send (8 AM to 10 AM KSA, i.e. GMT+3) to allow the recipient
  the full working day to act before a midnight close. If the deadline is a specific hour
  earlier in the day, move the send earlier to guarantee the window.

## 2. Urgency and scarcity that reads premium, not spammy

### What luxury and near-luxury brands do differently from discounters

This is the most important gap for the Maharat brand. The research returns a clear bifurcation.

Mass-market discounters use: "SALE ENDS MIDNIGHT!!!", countdown timers, all-caps subject
lines, percentage-off as the hero of every touchpoint, fake scarcity ("only 12 spots left",
when 12,000 spots exist).

Premium brands do not sell a price cut. They sell a window. The copy positions the offer as a
time-bound access event, not a price emergency. Key distinctions found across luxury and
premium brand copywriting research (sources: 303 London "10 Copywriting Techniques", Appnova
"8 Copywriting Essentials", Quikly "10 Examples of Promotional Messages", June 2026 search
synthesis):

1. Scarcity is real or it is not used. "Fifty made this season" is credible. "Sale ends
   midnight" is credible only if it actually ends at midnight and does not extend. Premium
   brands that run extensions destroy their credibility on the next campaign. The
   strategy-artifact already holds this line: the confirmed date from the brief is the date
   the offer closes, no extension.

2. The offer is a reward, not a rescue. A premium brand says "this is for our community, this
   summer, for a short window" not "we have leftover capacity, buy before it disappears." The
   Maharat frame already handles this: the summer moment is a genuine external seasonal
   context (longer free hours, self-development intent), not an invented occasion.

3. Urgency lives in the body and preheader, not the subject line. Premium subject lines name
   the offer or the value, not the deadline. The deadline is the preheader's job and the
   body's closing line. This keeps the subject from reading as a shouting discounter while
   still placing the deadline where the reader will find it. (See section 4 for subject-line
   mechanics.)

4. One deadline statement per email, not repeated three times in the same send. Repeating the
   deadline in the header, mid-body, and footer is a discounter pattern. State it once, calmly,
   specifically, and trust the reader.

5. The discount is the close, not the opening. The email opens on value (the skill, the
   library, what the reader gains). The discount or code appears in the second or third
   paragraph or in a dedicated PromoLine module, after the value case has been made. This
   sequence protects brand equity: the reader evaluates the product first, then learns the
   price is better than usual. The alternative (opening with the percentage) trains the reader
   to wait for deals.

6. "Ending tomorrow" done tastefully means stating the date, not the countdown. "The offer
   ends Sunday" reads premium. "24 hours left!!!" reads like a spam folder escape attempt.
   The Arabic equivalent: "العرض ينتهي يوم الأحد" is calm and credible. "لا يبقى لك إلا يوم
   واحد!!!" is the pattern to avoid entirely.

### Scarcity credibility rule (applies directly to E5, E6, E7)

Scarcity only works when buyers believe access is limited, meaning dates need to be real,
inventory claims need to be real, and the campaign needs to end when stated (source: Quikly
promotional messages synthesis, June 2026). For a subscription, "seats" are not scarce but the
promotional price is. The correct scarcity frame is price-time scarcity ("this price, for this
window") not artificial supply scarcity ("only N spots left"). Never invent a seat count.

## 3. Subject-line and preheader urgency: deliverability and spam avoidance

### Words and patterns that trigger spam filters in 2025 to 2026

Modern spam filters use machine learning rather than simple word lists, but certain patterns
still sharpen spam scores. High-risk patterns for promotional email subject lines (sources:
mailwarm.com spam word list 2025, warmforge.ai spam triggers, campaign cleaner 2026,
gocustomer.ai spam words guide, Vertical Response 2026 subject-line guide):

Urgency phrasing that is consistently flagged:
- "Act now", "Urgent", "Limited time offer", "Offer expires", "Now only", "Don't miss out"
- "Last chance" as a standalone phrase (especially in all-caps)
- "Ends tonight" paired with an exclamation mark
- "Final notice" (sounds like a bill collection threat)
- "Hurry", "Rush", "Act fast", "Before it's too late"

Financial/promotional phrases that compound the risk:
- "FREE" in all-caps (lowercase "free" scores lower but still elevates risk in promotional
  context)
- "% off" preceded by a large round number and no qualifier
- "Limited time" as a phrase, especially stacked with exclamation marks or all-caps
- "$$$" or other symbol repetition

Formatting signals that elevate spam score regardless of words:
- ALL CAPS in the subject line
- Multiple exclamation marks (!!!)
- Emoji combined with urgency phrasing

What the research recommends instead (and these align to the Maharat brand already):

- Replace "Last chance" with a specific date: "Offer ends Sunday" or "أحد, آخر يوم للعرض"
- Replace "Act now" with a named next step: "Choose your plan before Sunday" / "اختر خطتك قبل الأحد"
- Replace "Limited time offer" with the season and the occasion: "Summer of Skills, this week
  only" / "عرض مهارات، هذا الأسبوع فقط"
- Replace "Hurry" with a quiet statement of what closes: "The summer price closes [date]"
- Use specific time references over vague urgency: "Registration closes Friday" converts
  better than "ACT NOW: OFFER EXPIRES" and passes filters more reliably (source: spam
  guidance synthesis, June 2026)

### Subject-line formula for E5, E6, E7 that passes filters and stays premium

E5 announce: name the offer and the season in 3 to 6 words, no urgency word, no exclamation.
Pattern: "[season/occasion], [what you get or what opens]". Examples in register:
- AR: "صيف المهارات، ابدأ بخصم" (do not use tatweel; this is illustrative direction)
- EN: "Summer of Skills. Your offer is here."

E6 ending tomorrow: the subject carries the date or the qualifier "tomorrow", calmly. The
urgency is the fact itself, not the phrasing.
- AR: "العرض ينتهي غداً"
- EN: "The offer ends tomorrow"
This is a short, calm, factual subject. It is not a shout. It passes filters because it makes
no financial promise, no exclamation, no all-caps.

E7 last chance: the subject is the final, most specific statement of the close.
- AR: "آخر يوم للعرض الصيفي" (or with the confirmed closing day named)
- EN: "Today is the last day" or "The offer closes tonight"
Keep it 4 to 7 words. No "!!!". No "FREE". No "ACT NOW".

### Preheader as the urgency carrier

The preheader is where you put the deadline detail without risking the subject line. The
`insights.md` already confirmed this: "the preheader is where the urgency detail lives without
shouting in the subject." Research confirms this as a premium-brand pattern. The preheader
adds the layer the subject left open:

E5 subject: "Summer of Skills. Your offer is here." / preheader: "[X]% off the entry plan.
Offer ends [day, date]. The full library, one plan."
E6 subject: "The offer ends tomorrow." / preheader: "The summer price on the Maharat entry
plan closes [day]. One step, no commitment after."
E7 subject: "Today is the last day." / preheader: "The offer ends at midnight [day date].
Choose your plan now and keep the summer price."

The preheader's deadline information is specific (day and date in Western numerals, no
estimate), calm, and adds one new piece of value not in the subject (the time of close, the
plan name, or the scope of what they get).

## 4. Discount presentation: how to show a percentage and a code tastefully

### The hierarchy: value first, deal second

Premium brand discount email structure, confirmed across multiple sources (Voucherify premium
promotions research, Quikly examples, Drip promotional email synthesis, June 2026):

Paragraph 1: the value case. What the learner gets, why it matters this summer, in empowering
language. This is not the discount paragraph. The reader evaluates the product at full value
first.

Paragraph 2: the offer. The discount and the code appear here, stated plainly: "[X]% off the
[plan name]. Use [CODE]. Offer ends [day date]." One sentence for the percentage, one for the
code, one for the deadline. No stacked hyperbole around it. The offer speaks for itself.

The PromoLine module: in the Maharat system this is the dedicated visual element for the code
and deadline. It belongs between the body and the CTA button, not at the top of the email.
Placing it at the top (as discounters do) turns the email into a coupon, not a brand
communication. Placing it after the value argument means the reader has already decided they
want the product, and the code is the reward.

### How to show the percentage without cheapening the brand

From the research (Voucherify, Branding Strategy Insider, Appnova, June 2026):

Premium brands protect equity in price promotions in 3 ways:

1. Keep the discount moderate. A 70 percent cut on a premium product tells the customer the
   original price was inflated. The implied message of an extreme discount is "this product was
   not worth its price." A discount in the range of 20 to 40 percent on a subscription product
   is calibrated to reward action without signaling desperation. (The placeholder in the
   strategy-artifact is 30 percent off. If confirmed at the gate, this sits in the right range
   for a premium subscription.)

2. Frame the saving in absolute value, not only percentage. "Save [X] [currency] on your first
   year" can feel more concrete and less coupon-y than "[X]% off", especially when the
   percentage is a round number that looks like every other sale. Use whichever framing the
   brief's confirmed numbers make most compelling: if the absolute saving is larger and more
   impressive than the percentage, use the absolute figure. If the percentage is cleaner, use
   that. Do not stack both ("save [X]% AND [Y] riyals") because that is a discounter pattern.

3. State the original price in context, not as a strikethrough. A literal crossed-out price
   visually signals a bargain bin. The premium alternative: "The entry plan is normally [price].
   This summer, it is [sale price]." Stated in prose, not in a visual strikethrough. The
   difference is perceptual: the reader processes the comparison intellectually rather than
   viscerally associating it with a clearance rack.

### The promo code: how to present it

Make the code easy to read and copy, styled plainly, in a monospace or distinct type treatment
that signals "this is functional" without decorating it into noise. The code sits in the
PromoLine module between body and CTA. One line: "Use code [CODE] at checkout." Do not repeat
the code in the subject line or preheader; it belongs in the body where there is room to make
the copy scannable.

For Arabic readers: the code itself (e.g. SUM30) stays in Latin characters even in the Arabic
email, because promotional codes are typed as-is. Wrap it with a brief Arabic frame: "استخدم
الرمز [CODE] عند الدفع." RTL rendering must not disrupt the code string. Test this in the
email client before final approval.

### Maintaining credibility as a value-led brand during a promotion

The research returns a consistent warning (Branding Strategy Insider, Appnova, Retail Brew
2022, June 2026 synthesis): a premium brand that discounts loses more than it gains if the
promotion trains the audience to wait. Three guardrails apply directly to Maharat:

Guardrail 1: the promotion must be a real occasion. The summer window is a genuine seasonal
moment in the GCC (see section 5). "This summer" is a credible occasion; "we happen to have
a sale" is not. Lead on the season, not on the price.

Guardrail 2: the promotion must actually end. If E7 says the offer closes today and the code
still works next week, the next sale is worth 0 in credibility terms. Operationally, this
means the code must be deactivated at the confirmed close. The compliance gate (open item 1 in
the strategy-artifact) blocks the sale tail until the confirmed date is set. Once set, it
must be honored.

Guardrail 3: the subscription is the win, the price is the occasion. The body copy never
implies the subscription is only worthwhile because of the discount. The value case in E5
must be completable as a paragraph even if the discount line is removed. The discount
accelerates a decision the reader was already going to make, it does not rescue a product the
reader would otherwise pass on. This is the frame the brand voice requires and it is also
what actually protects brand equity in future sends.

## 5. Arabic and GCC audience notes

### What is specific to this audience for promotional email

From the research (memob.com GCC consumer behavior 2026, tamara.co Decoding the GCC Consumer
2024, shamiltranslation.com Arabic-English marketing 2026, June 2026 search synthesis):

1. Arabic subject lines stand out in a GCC inbox dominated by English marketing email. An
   Arabic subject line from a brand that matches the reader's first language is a positive
   differentiator. The `insights.md` subject-line data from the 9 reference sequences confirms
   this holds for Maharat's own sends: the brand already uses Arabic-primary subjects with
   measured, short phrasing. Keep that practice across E5, E6, E7.

2. GCC consumers are active participants in promotional events. Research from a GCC consumer
   study (consultancy-me.com, 2026) found that in Saudi Arabia, Back to School and Black
   Friday participation rates hit 96 percent. Summer promotions with a genuine occasion (the
   back-to-school and post-Eid downtime period in July) land in a seasonally receptive window.
   The strategy-artifact's proposed close of about July 6 sits inside this window. Eid al-Adha
   falls before the campaign; the post-Eid calm is a period when GCC consumers shift from
   festive spending to practical purchases and self-improvement.

3. GCC consumers respond to urgency when it is specific and honest, not when it is fabricated.
   The research from 2024 to 2026 consistently shows that "seeking deals and promotions" is a
   top money-saving behavior (47 percent of respondents, tamara.co 2024), but fake scarcity
   or extended "last chance" windows erode trust disproportionately in a market where
   word-of-mouth and social proof are strong.

4. Soft-sell outperforms hard-sell in the GCC, particularly in digital channels. Ramadan
   marketing research (infobip.com, aivensoft.com, mcgmasters.com, June 2026) consistently
   finds that empathy, value-orientation, and community framing outperform aggressive
   promotional copy. The same cultural deference applies in summer: the tone stays warm and
   inviting, never commanding or pressurizing. This maps directly to the Maharat brand voice.

5. Send timing for the GCC. Morning local sends (8 AM to 10 AM KSA, GMT+3) are already in
   the sequence-standards. For the sale tail specifically, E7 (last-chance, day of close) can
   also benefit from a second touchpoint in the late afternoon (2 PM to 4 PM KSA) if the
   platform supports two sends on the same day to the same list without unsubscribe risk. This
   is a tactical option, not a requirement. If a second send on E7 day is considered, it must
   be a different subject line and shorter body, framing the actual close hour rather than
   restating the morning email. Two sends on one day is acceptable for a genuine same-day
   close; it is not acceptable as a standard practice across the arc.

6. Mobile-first. The GCC audience is highly mobile: 47 percent of UAE back-to-school purchases
   in 2025 were on mobile (memob.com). This means the sale tail emails must render cleanly on
   mobile at every point, the PromoLine code must be easy to tap-to-copy, and the CTA button
   must be thumb-reachable. The design-qa gate covers this, but the copywriter should keep
   body paragraphs short knowing most readers will skim on a phone.

### Urgency framing in Arabic: tone calibration

Translated urgency phrases that feel native and premium in Gulf Arabic (Modern Standard Arabic
with Gulf register):

For "ending tomorrow": "العرض ينتهي غداً" (statement of fact, calm), preferred over "لا تفوّت
الفرصة" (do-not-miss framing, which edges toward hype).

For "last chance": "آخر يوم" (last day, neutral statement) or "آخر فرصة" (last chance,
acceptable because it is a factual description of a real close, not an invented one),
preferred over "اغتنم الفرصة الآن قبل فوات الأوان" (grab the chance now before it is too
late, which is deadline anxiety phrasing and conflicts with the empowering frame).

For the offer itself: "خصم [X]% على خطتك" or "ابدأ صيفك بخصم [X]%" (lead with the seasonal
framing, then the percentage). Avoid "احصل على خصم هائل" (get a huge discount, which is the
discounter register).

The closing CTA on E7: "اختر خطتك قبل انتهاء العرض" (choose your plan before the offer ends)
is the correct register. "اشترك الآن" (subscribe now) is also clean and direct. Both fit the
CTA ladder from `insights.md`.

## Apply to E5 / E6 / E7: cheat-sheet

What follows is the decision-layer summary a copywriter can apply directly. Every item below
reconciles with the Maharat brand voice. Conflicts between common growth-hack tactics and the
brand are called out.

### Structure

E5 (announce): value paragraph, then offer paragraph (percentage + code + deadline), then
one CTA. Body length: 3 short paragraphs. Tone: informative, warm, the offer is news. Enable
PromoLine with confirmed values.

E6 (ending tomorrow): shorter. One value reminder sentence, one offer restatement sentence,
one deadline sentence, one CTA. Body length: 2 paragraphs, the second being the deadline.
Tone: calm, factual, the urgency is the fact.

E7 (last chance): shortest. One sentence: what they get. One sentence: the deadline, with the
specific day and close hour. One CTA. Body length: 1 to 2 paragraphs. Tone: direct, final,
no apology, no new offer, no added inducement.

### Urgency framing

Use: specific dates ("ends Sunday [date]"), specific times on E7 ("ends at midnight"),
calm declarative sentences, the preheader as the urgency vessel.

Avoid: "act now", "don't miss out", "hurry", "limited time offer" as a phrase, exclamation
marks, all-caps, "LAST CHANCE" in the subject, countdown clock GIFs (they render badly in
email and read as discounter), stacked deadlines in every paragraph.

Growth-hack pattern that conflicts with the brand: putting the percentage-off in the subject
line. Common tactic ("30% off ends tonight") but it (a) risks spam classification and (b)
positions the email as a coupon, not a brand communication. On-brand alternative: keep the
percentage in the body PromoLine; make the subject carry the season or the value ("Summer of
Skills, this is your week").

Growth-hack pattern that conflicts with the brand: countdown timers and animated deadline
clocks. These convert in mass-market ecommerce but look cheap in a premium learning context.
On-brand alternative: state the close time in plain text, once, in the preheader and the last
sentence of the body.

Growth-hack pattern that conflicts with the brand: extending the "last chance" window. If E7
says the offer closes today, it closes today. The next campaign depends on this being true.

### Discount presentation

Lead with value, land the code in the PromoLine between body and CTA. Frame the discount as a
seasonal reward ("a summer offer for the Maharat community") not as a rescue ("we are giving
you a deal because you have not subscribed yet"). The absolute saving can be used instead of
or alongside the percentage if the confirmed numbers make the absolute figure more impressive.
Do not use a visual strikethrough on the price.

For the promo code in Arabic email: "استخدم الرمز [CODE] عند الدفع" is the correct Arabic
wrapper. Test RTL rendering of the Latin-character code before final approval.

### Subject line formulas (spam-safe, premium register)

E5: "[Season/occasion noun phrase]" + one comma or period + "[what opens or what you get]"
- 4 to 7 words, no exclamation, no percentage in subject.
- AR pattern: "صيف المهارات، عرضك الآن" or campaign-specific variant
- EN pattern: "Summer of Skills. Your offer is here."

E6: plain statement of tomorrow's close, 4 to 6 words.
- AR: "العرض ينتهي غداً"
- EN: "The offer ends tomorrow"

E7: plain statement of today's close, 4 to 7 words.
- AR: "آخر يوم للعرض الصيفي" or with the confirmed day named
- EN: "Today is the last day" or "The offer closes tonight"

### Preheader as the urgency carrier

Every E5 to E7 preheader carries: the specific deadline + the plan scope + one short value
reminder. The subject never repeats in the preheader.

E5 preheader model: "[X]% off the entry plan. Offer ends [day date]. The full library, one
plan." / "[X]% على خطة المبتدئ. العرض ينتهي [day date]. المكتبة كاملة باشتراك واحد."

E6 preheader model: "The offer on the Maharat entry plan ends [day]. Choose your plan before
it closes." / "خصم المهارات ينتهي [day]. اختر خطتك قبل أن ينتهي."

E7 preheader model: "The offer ends at [hour], [day] [date]. Your plan, your skills, yours to
keep." / "العرض ينتهي الساعة [hour] [day] [date]. خطتك ومهاراتك معك."

### Arabic and GCC tone notes

The deadline is stated as a factual sentence, not an anxiety sentence. "آخر يوم" not "لا تضيع
الفرصة". The subscription frame is the learner's gain ("مهارة تبقى معك") not a fear of loss
("لا تفوّت العرض"). These two are not the same psychologically: both are urgency, but one is
empowering and one is deficit-framed. The brand always uses the first.

GCC summer context is real and usable: the window after Eid al-Adha into early July is a
genuine self-development and back-to-school ramp-up period in Saudi Arabia and the GCC.
This is not an invented seasonality; it is a documented consumer behavior pattern. The copy
can reference "this summer" and "before the new term" as natural, honest anchors.
