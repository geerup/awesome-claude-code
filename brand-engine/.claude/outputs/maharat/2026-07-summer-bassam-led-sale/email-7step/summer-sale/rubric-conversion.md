# Conversion scoring rubric (score each email /10 per metric, target >= 8 on every metric)

Shared standard for the copy-doctor agents (copywriter-ar, copywriter-en) and the reviewer
(scorer). Every one of the 7 emails is scored in BOTH languages. An email is "done" only when all
8 metrics are >= 8/10 in both AR and EN. Below 8 on any metric returns a specific fix to the author.

Premium-conversion principle: high conversion here does NOT mean hype. No exclamation marks, no
all-caps, no spam words ("act now", "hurry", "amazing", "unbelievable"), no emoji. The pull comes
from a real curiosity gap, a specific benefit, concrete nouns, and an open loop the body closes.
This is the Thmanyah register: a smart friend who respects the reader. Wow, then click, then the
body delivers on the promise.

## The 8 metrics (each /10)

1. Subject stopping power. Would this stop the scroll in a busy inbox and earn the open? Real
   curiosity or a sharp, specific benefit. Premium, never robotic, never a cold slogan. Front-loaded.
2. Subject set strength. The primary plus 2 alts are 3 genuinely different, strong angles (not
   padding), each mobile-safe (about 30 to 40 chars), each clickable on its own.
3. Preheader pull. Adds a new layer to the subject (never repeats it), compounds the curiosity or
   states the payoff/deadline, makes the open feel worth it.
4. Headline hook. The first in-body line lands the promise hard and pulls the reader into the body.
5. Body persuasion. Carries one real, specific piece of value, flows logically to a single CTA,
   reads effortlessly, and makes the click feel obvious. Conversion-oriented, not descriptive.
6. Voice and premium tone. Thmanyah register, empowering never deficit, natural never robotic or
   translated-sounding, confident without hype. No customer-facing jargon (no "platform").
7. CTA strength and fit. One CTA, an active verb that fits the email's stage, clear on what happens
   next, and consistent with the escalation across the sequence.
8. Claims, mechanics, parity. Only cleared facts (Bassam authority line, 20 lessons, the free first
   lesson; only the 3 cleared instructors; sale terms held as placeholders). House style clean (no
   em dash, no tatweel, Western numerals). AR reads natively; AR and EN carry the same promise.

## Hard constraints (carry over, non-negotiable)
- Do NOT use the word "platform" or "منصة" in customer-facing copy. Use "Maharat" / "on Maharat" /
  "مهارات" / "على مهارات".
- One CTA per email. No price in copy. Sale terms (30%, SUM30, 2026-07-06) stay PLACEHOLDER, HELD.
- No invented claims, instructors, lessons, counts, or accreditation.
- No em dashes, no tatweel, Western numerals only, RTL-safe.

## Specific requester notes to apply
- E1: not "a look you can do yourself". Use the "makeup looks you can do yourself" direction.
- E2: "It is technique, not products" is too robotic/cold. Make it warm and compelling.
- Every subject and alt should make the reader feel "wow, this is for me", and click. The body must
  then deliver on that promise.

## Scoring output (per email, both languages)
For each email: a table of the 8 metric scores /10, a one-line justification for any score below 9,
and an explicit fix for anything below 8. End with a sequence-level note on arc, escalation, and
bilingual parity.
