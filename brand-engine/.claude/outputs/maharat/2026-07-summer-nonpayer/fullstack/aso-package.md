## 1. Keyword Research

This section documents the keyword map for App Store and Google Play, Arabic and English. It is
desk research grounded in confirmed class content, audience definition, and store mechanics. It
is not validated against live search volume or ranking data; that validation waits on ASO tool
approval from Ahmed.

### 1.1 Research rationale

The campaign angle is breadth plus credibility: this summer you can build a real skill, in a
field you choose, taught by people who set the standard in that field. Unlike a single-instructor
campaign, the store listing must serve both the user arriving with a specific field interest
(music, cooking, acting, makeup, business, styling, marketing) and the user arriving at the
platform level (Arabic self-development app, online skills, Gulf learning platform). The keyword
map carries both layers.

Intent zones:
- Platform-level and self-development: Arabic-speaking adults searching for skill-building,
  self-development apps, online courses in Arabic, or learning apps in the GCC. This is the
  always-on, campaign-agnostic layer that serves the listing year-round.
- Per-field aspiration: users with a specific domain in mind arriving through paid or organic
  during the summer flight. Each of the seven confirmed fields maps to real, published masterclass
  intent: music performance and career, cooking and professional chef skills, acting and
  expression, makeup artistry, business building, celebrity styling, and marketing leadership.
- Summer and seasonal: intent around self-development in the summer, using free time to build
  skills, seasonal learning. A weaker but real signal in the GCC summer window.
- Free intro chapter: "first lesson free" and equivalent phrasing is a search-intent signal and
  a conversion lever simultaneously. It is grounded in the confirmed chapter 1 free feature
  across all seven classes.

Arabic-first throughout. English variant list researched independently for bilingual GCC users
and EN-locale store listings.

### 1.2 App Store field and character limits (applied throughout)

- Title: 30 characters max, shared character logic. Each locale has its own title string.
- Subtitle: 30 characters max per locale.
- Keyword field: 100 characters per locale. Comma-separated, no spaces after commas. No terms
  that duplicate the title or subtitle (the algorithm already indexes those). No repetition across
  locales (the algorithm deduplicates App Store-wide).
- Description: 4,000 characters max per locale. Not directly indexed by the App Store algorithm
  for ranking, but visible to users and affects click-through and conversion.

### 1.3 Google Play field and character limits (applied throughout)

- Title: 30 characters max.
- Short description: 80 characters max. Indexed for ranking.
- Long description: 4,000 characters max. Indexed. Keyword placement in the first 167 characters
  is highest-weight. Keywords must appear naturally; no separate keyword field.

All character counts apply to rendered text (Arabic characters count as 1 each despite being
multi-byte in storage). Counts must be verified precisely by copywriter-ar for Arabic strings and
by copywriter-en for English strings before any string is final.

## 4. Store A/B Experiment Plan

Experiments are planned and prepped here. They do not run until the human gate approves and
store console access is confirmed (OPEN ITEM). Each experiment tests one variable at a time
and is tied to the strategy-artifact angle (breadth plus credibility, summer self-development).
Each success measure is linked to the primary success metric from the strategy artifact:
paid subscription conversions attributable to the campaign. An install-rate uplift with no
downstream subscription uplift is a signal, not a win.

### Experiment 1: Subtitle angle test (App Store, AR locale)

- Variable tested: subtitle copy angle.
- Variant A (control): platform breadth subtitle. Direction for copywriter-ar: something in
  the register of "مهارات من نخبة العرب" (Skills from the Arab world's elite).
- Variant B (treatment): summer season angle. Direction for copywriter-ar: something in the
  register of "صيف المهارات. ابدأ مجاناً" (Summer of Skills. Start for free). This hooks the
  seasonal intent and the free entry in 30 characters.
- Hypothesis: during the campaign flight, the season-plus-free-entry subtitle will lift
  install conversion rate (store page views to downloads) in the AR locale among users arriving
  from summer self-development intent, because the seasonal hook plus the free first chapter is
  a lower-friction entry signal than the platform-breadth line alone.
- Success measure: install conversion rate (store page view to download) in the App Store AR
  locale, compared across variants. Read downstream: any install uplift must trace to
  subscription conversion to count as a win against the primary success metric.
- Duration: minimum 7 days of statistically equivalent traffic before reading results. Run
  through at least half the confirmed campaign flight.
- Gate: App Store Connect product page optimization. Requires store console access confirmed.

### Experiment 2: First screenshot angle test (App Store, both locales)

- Variable tested: the hero visual in screenshot frame 1 (and its copy overlay).
- Variant A (control): summer aspiration frame. Abstract brand-constant visual, emerald accent,
  headline copy in the register of the summer breadth angle (many fields, one platform, one summer).
- Variant B (treatment): breadth grid frame. The seven-field card mosaic (frame 2 content from
  the main sequence becomes the first screenshot). The hypothesis is that a proof-first opening
  that shows many fields reduces the "which field?" hesitation for new users before they tap
  through.
- Hypothesis: showing breadth (many fields) as the very first screenshot will outperform the
  single aspiration frame for cold traffic arriving from the acquisition campaigns, because the
  breadth immediately answers the consideration question for a user who has not yet committed to
  a field.
- Success measure: tap-through rate from search results to the store listing page (impression to
  page view) and then install conversion rate. Linked to subscription conversion downstream.
- Duration: minimum 7 days. Run for the same window as experiment 1.
- Gate: App Store Connect product page optimization. Requires console access confirmed.

### Experiment 3: Google Play short description lead test (AR locale)

- Variable tested: the lead sentence of the Google Play short description (80 characters,
  highest-indexed field after the title).
- Variant A (control): platform-led. Direction for copywriter-ar: something in the register of

## 5. Reviews Response Policy

### 5.1 Principles

Every public review response is customer-facing. No response template goes live without passing
brand-qa-reviewer. The policy is grounded in the Maharat voice: clear, modern, confident,
empowering, never stiff or defensive. The brand sounds like a smart, respectful friend, not
a corporate helpdesk.

Mechanics:
- No em dashes anywhere.
- No tatweel or kashida.
- Western numerals only.
- RTL-correct for Arabic responses.
- Thank the gesture of the review, not personal data about the reviewer.
- Empowering register: acknowledge, move to what can be done, invite further discovery.
- No roadmap. No Skill Path launch announcements. No unannounced plans. No upcoming instructor
  or class announcements.
- No accreditation implication: if a user asks whether the completion certificate is accredited,
  the response states clearly that it is a Maharat completion certificate and does not imply
  external accreditation.
- No price commitments or promotion confirmations in public responses. Direct pricing questions
  to maharat.com/ar/plans (Arabic) or maharat.com/en/plans (English). Do not confirm or deny
  any promotion.
- No naming of the four non-nameable instructors (Rahma Riad, Sami Al Jaber, Mona Ataya, Mo
  Islam) in any review response.

### 5.2 Summer campaign-specific response tone

During the campaign flight, responses may acknowledge the summer skills theme warmly (for
example, referencing the breadth of fields available this summer) but must not:
- Announce a specific summer promo unless Ahmed has confirmed one publicly.
- Reference any Skill Paths launch date or upcoming features.
- Name any instructor not confirmed as public-nameable.

### 5.3 Response routing by review type

Positive review (4 to 5 stars):
- Respond warmly and briefly. Acknowledge what they valued. Invite them to try another field
  or explore more of the roster.
- Arabic template direction: something in the register of "شكراً على رأيك. يسعدنا أن تجد ما
  تبحث عنه على ماهارات. استكشف مجالات أخرى وابدأ مهارة جديدة." No em dash. No tatweel.
- Escalate if: the review makes a public claim about the platform that is factually incorrect
  (for example an accreditation claim). Do not correct a false public claim without legal or
  comms review first.

Negative review (1 to 3 stars), technical issue (crash, playback, login):
- Acknowledge the issue plainly. Direct to the support channel. State that the team is looking
  into it. Do not speculate on the cause.
- Support channel: OPEN ITEM. The confirmed support URL or contact path is not in the brief.
  Placeholder: direct users to "تواصل معنا من خلال الموقع" (contact us through the site) until
  the support path is confirmed. Do not invent a support URL.
- Escalate if: billing, payment, or subscription error is alleged. Route to Ahmed before any
  public response.

Negative review, content or instructor feedback:
- Acknowledge the feedback respectfully. Do not argue. Do not promise curriculum changes or
  new content. Do not name an instructor negatively or amplify a negative claim publicly.
- Escalate to Ahmed if: the review alleges factual errors in a class, or makes a public
  complaint about a named instructor.

Negative review, pricing or value:
- Acknowledge the perspective. Direct to the plans page. Do not confirm or deny any promotion
  or discount. Do not state a price not publicly confirmed.
- Escalate if: the review references a specific pricing commitment or a promo that does not
  match anything publicly confirmed.

Review implying certificate accreditation:
- This is a compliance-adjacent trigger. Respond once, clearly, in brief: the certificate is a
  Maharat completion certificate. It does not imply external accreditation. Do not expand.
- Escalate to Ahmed if the review is featured prominently or if the user presses publicly.

Spam or abusive reviews:
- Do not engage publicly. Flag the review for platform reporting. Escalate to Ahmed.

### 5.4 What never appears in a public review response

- Any implication that the Maharat completion certificate is accredited or recognized by an
  external body.
- Any mention of unannounced Skill Paths, launch dates, or upcoming classes or instructors not
  yet confirmed public.
- Any specific promotion, discount, or trial not yet confirmed publicly by Ahmed.
- Any roadmap item or product timeline.
- Personal or sensitive data about the reviewer.
- Names of non-nameable instructors (Rahma Riad, Sami Al Jaber, Mona Ataya, Mo Islam).
- Em dashes. Tatweel. Eastern Arabic-Indic numerals.

### 5.5 Escalation threshold

Route to Ahmed for a decision before responding publicly when:
- A review makes a claim (accreditation, pricing, content accuracy) that could create legal or
  reputational exposure if the response is mishandled.
- A review mentions a named instructor negatively or in a misleading context.
- A review alleges a billing or subscription error.
- A review is featured prominently in the listing or has a high helpfulness score (high
  visibility raises the stakes of the response).
- Any doubt exists about whether a response crosses a guardrail. When in doubt, escalate.
