The first influencer department: zero budget, ROI-positive month one.

When I joined Mindvalley in 2017, influencer marketing did not exist as a department - ad hoc creator relationships, no programme, no measurement. I built it on relationship-based agreements instead of paid placements: a network that reached 10M+ people, ROI-positive from month one, on zero placement budget. 01 · The problem

A perfect category, no programme.

Influencer work at Mindvalley was ad hoc: creator relationships with no programme, no measurement, no relationship model - despite personal growth being perfectly aligned with the creator economy.

The programme had to be cost-effective, measurable, and scalable without a placement budget. Paying for posts was the industry default; we did not have the budget appetite for it - and paid advocacy converts worse in a trust category.

02 · The calls

Three decisions did the work.

The zero-cost model. Instead of paying for posts, negotiate relationship-based agreements giving creators access to Mindvalley's most valuable asset: its faculty. Creators could interview Vishen Lakhiani and access quests and masterclasses, then present content they genuinely engaged with.
RejectedPaid placements - explicit in the source.
WhyAuthentic advocacy converts in a trust category; a rate card does not.
Concentrate where conversion was proven. YouTube for long-form and organic conversions, Instagram for micro content and lives, Facebook for promotable warm-audience partner posts; bloggers, Reddit and Pinterest activated only strategically.
OKRs over reach. Three measurable objectives - awareness (5% monthly lift in shares/mentions), traffic (5% lift in visits from creator channels), leads (1,000 masterclass sign-ups per campaign) - and every partnership evaluated against them.

03 · The run

Campaigns, and a way to attribute the unattributable.

  • #iAmEnough. The Uncompromised Life campaign.
  • Superbrain by Jim Kwik. Creator brief, tracking links, coordinated timeline.
  • Event coverage. Treated A-Fest, Mindvalley University and Mindvalley Summit as content-production moments for creators.
Attribution by proxy

To attribute the unattributable, we tracked YouTube subscriber spikes on partner-video publish days. The day "Upgrade Your Consciousness" published, Mindvalley's channel saw a 9.2% increase in new subscribers.

The template that carried forward

This model became the blueprint for the 48-creator Always-On network I later built at Canonical.

Honest caveat

The source states the tradeoff plainly: relationship-based programmes are slower to build and harder to scale quickly than paid placement - but significantly more durable and more effective per impression. ROI is real but its calculation basis needs stating, since placements cost $0 and the true costs were team time and faculty access.
