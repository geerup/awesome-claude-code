# Data & AI Masters - 4,731 registrations on organic promotion

**Rank: 4 of 5 · Pillars: P3 (defensible demand gen), P2 (technical audience)**

## Hook

I conceived and produced a virtual event for Canonical's data and AI infrastructure products that pulled 4,731 registrations across 52 countries - promoted through organic social and email that ran at nearly double the benchmark open rate.

## Context

In 2024 Canonical needed awareness and qualified pipeline for its data and AI infrastructure line (Charmed Kafka, Charmed OpenSearch, Charmed MongoDB, MicroCloud) in a market dominated by hyperscalers - without a large event budget and without a household brand name in the data space.

## Tension

The products deserved a seat at the table; the marketing had to earn one. Registrations had to come from channels Canonical already owned.

## Mandate

I conceived and produced Data & AI Masters and built its full marketing programme: organic social, LinkedIn event pages, email, and cross-channel content.

## Decisions

- **Each channel got one job.** Social for awareness and trust, LinkedIn for community-native conversion, email for urgency and close. The source states the operating belief plainly: virtual events succeed or fail at the promotion layer.
- NEEDS_HUMAN: whether paid promotion was considered and rejected (the site claims "zero paid spend"; the case source does not state the fork).

## Execution

- Organic social promotion: **153,000 impressions at 7.5% engagement rate** against a 5% benchmark, targeted at developer and infrastructure audiences.
- LinkedIn event page: **426 registrations, 249 day-1 attendees, 147 hours of in-platform watch time**.
- Email: **120,000 deliveries at a 20.8% average open rate** against Canonical's 12% monthly benchmark; the last-call email hit 23.5%.
- Produced **20+ reusable video sessions**, repurposed for demand gen, social and sales enablement through Q4 2024.

## Outcome

**4,731 registrations, 1,532 live attendees, 52 countries** (Marketing Townhall, October 3, 2024). Post-event, 92.6% of attendees said they would attend such an event in person - an unusually strong intent signal from a virtual-first audience.
